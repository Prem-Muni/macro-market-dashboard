HOW THE SCORE IS BUILT

16 health points per ETF

Six shared macro plus ten ETF-specific signals. Each verified scored state earns one health point if it is not bearish; bearish earns zero. Neutral indicators can earn points.

MACD (12,26,9) is scored when its line is at or above its signal; Fear & Greed remains information only.

DAY-TO-DAY INTERPRETATION

Higher = improving

Change is the latest Market Health Score minus the earlier score. +2 or more over five sessions = Improving; −2 or less = Deteriorating; −1 to +1 = Stable.

These are changes in scored conditions, not the ETF's percentage return.

INCOMPLETE DATA

Show the range, not a guess

A missing scored reading contributes an unknown zero or one. The dashboard displays a score interval and a definite regime only if every possible score belongs to that regime.

Sixteen available inputs is coverage, not proof of data accuracy or forecasting performance.

WHAT ISN'T A SCORE

Context remains separate

Market Confirmation (volatility, credit, breadth, price), swing environment, average prices, Fear & Greed and historical price/risk alerts help interpret the score but do not add extra health points.

Some signals may overlap—for example, trend, RSI, MACD and breadth.

EVIDENCE & LIMITATIONS

How much confidence should we have?

The current 0–5 / 6–10 / 11–16 bands are a chosen experimental policy, not validated statistical probabilities. Historical reconstructed scores can differ from the values that were actually displayed on past trading days. Sources may be delayed, and constituent-based breadth can be estimated and subject to survivorship bias.

Historical comparison: older 15-point entries retain their original denominators. They cannot be interpreted as verified 16-point observations. When evaluating signal performance, compare forward SPY/QQQ returns, regime frequency and hit rates on separately dated samples before changing cutoffs.

WHY THE THRESHOLDS DIFFER

Same 15 indicators. Two Risk-On cutoffs.

This page records what the model does, why the threshold was chosen, and what the historical evidence actually showed. It is intended as a durable reference for future reviews and anyone with whom the dashboard is shared.

Each scored bearish condition contributes one point. A lower score means fewer selected risk conditions. Risk-On is a market-regime label, not a prediction, a guarantee, or a direct buy instruction.

Why: QQQ's growth-heavy, concentrated composition makes its swings different from SPY's. Score-5 outcomes were generally less unfavorable than SPY's, although still weaker than QQQ 0–4. We deliberately allow 5 as a borderline Risk-On policy choice, not a fully confirmed bullish signal.

DECISION RATIONALE

Why we made this distinction

1 · Both use the same scoring framework

Six shared macro indicators plus nine index-specific indicators. We do not add or remove indicators for QQQ; only the Risk-On classification boundary differs.

2 · A score of 5 is not “strong bullish”

In QQQ it is classified Risk-On for usability, but explicitly labeled borderline cautious. We still consider five-day trend, breadth, volatility, and market confirmation before making decisions.

3 · This is a judgment call, not proof

Reconstructed 20-month results do not show that QQQ 0–5 improves the 0–4 baseline. We accept the extra uncertainty as a chosen, more permissive growth-market threshold and will revisit it as new data accumulates.

QQQ tracks roughly 100 large non-financial Nasdaq-listed companies and is technology- and growth-heavy, not exclusively technology stocks. Its higher historical volatility is context for the policy choice, but volatility by itself is not evidence that adding score 5 improves subsequent returns.

How to interpret the decision responsibly

Important: This is a discretionary classification based on preliminary, reconstructed daily-bar research—not proof that QQQ score 5 provides an advantage. Five-session return windows overlap; the sample is short; historical breadth is estimated using today's constituents (survivorship bias); prices omit dividends, slippage, and costs. Selecting a cutoff after looking at the data also introduces selection bias. Risk-On does not mean risk-free, and the separate Swing Environment label may disagree because it applies additional conditions. Reassess with more periods and genuine out-of-sample data.
