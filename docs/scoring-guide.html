Market Health Score · Higher is healthier

Both benchmarks use the same 16-point scale: 0–5 Risk-Off, 6–10 Neutral, 11–16 Risk-On. Each scored indicator contributes 1 health point when it is not bearish and 0 when bearish. Neutral signals count as non-bearish, so this is a health score—not a count of strictly bullish votes.

Score calculation: Market Health = 16 − current bearish signal count. A higher daily change is improving; a lower daily change is deteriorating. With missing data, the website shows a range and will not assume absent inputs are healthy.

Six shared macro signals plus ten index-specific signals are now scored, including MACD(12,26,9). MACD line at/above its signal earns one health point; below it earns zero. Five individual SMA rows and external Fear & Greed remain informational only. The November 20 baseline and historical records retain their underlying bearish counts; the website retains their original 15-point denominator when displaying older records.

Research caveat: The new unified 0–5 / 6–10 / 11–16 classification has not been revalidated using forward returns. The earlier research sections below are archived analysis of the old bearish-score thresholds and should not be treated as evidence for the new bands.

16th scored input: Daily MACD (12, 26, 9)

SPY / QQQ each receive 1 Market Health point when the completed daily MACD line is at or above its 9-period signal line, otherwise 0. Missing MACD data creates an incomplete score interval. MACD here is a momentum confirmation, not a prediction; no Fear & Greed score is included.

Historical comparability: Old 15-point archive entries and the earlier backtest were created without a MACD score, and must not be reported as verified 16-point observations.

HOW TO READ IT

How the bearish score works

The live 15-point website uses SPY Risk-On 0–4 and QQQ Risk-On 0–5. QQQ 5 is borderline cautious; these are research-policy thresholds, not validated trading recommendations.

SPY · RISK-ON0–4 / 15

SPY CAUTION 5–8 · RISK-OFF 9–15.

QQQ · RISK-ON0–5 / 15

QQQ score 5 is borderline cautious. QQQ CAUTION 6–8 · RISK-OFF 9–15.

POLICY RATIONALE15 signals

Different Risk-On thresholds, same scoring indicators. Read the decision record and historical evidence.

+1 or 0—not a weighted average

Each scored signal contributes 1 bearish point only in its BEARISH state. BULLISH, CAUTION, FLAT, LAGGING and STABLE each contribute 0. Some non-scored rows can be red without changing the total.

Missing data is not bullish

If a scored feed is unavailable, the dashboard displays a possible score interval, such as 10–11/15. It shows a definite regime only when every possible score belongs to the same regime; otherwise it says INCOMPLETE.

Why there's an original 14-point number

The reference score removes the new RSI(14) point but keeps the previous 14 indicators, including the original 50D/200D Trend. The old score uses its own bands: 0–4 Risk-On, 5–7 Caution, and 8–14 Risk-Off.

+1 max

Daily USD index/proxy change is positive.

OTHER OUTCOMES · 0

Flat → CAUTION; falling → BULLISH. “DIV” is a same-direction comparison with SPY, not a second penalty.

10Y Yield

Daily direction of the 10-year Treasury yield proxy, often associated with discount-rate pressure.

+1 max
BEARISH · +1

Daily 10-year yield change is positive.

OTHER OUTCOMES · 0

Flat → CAUTION; falling → BULLISH. The “DIV” annotation compares its direction with SPY.

04

Credit · HYG / IEF

HYG represents high-yield corporate bonds; IEF represents intermediate Treasury bonds. Relative daily performance is a simple credit-risk appetite check.

+1 max
BEARISH · +1

HYG one-day percentage return is lower than IEF one-day percentage return.

OTHER OUTCOMES · 0

HYG return equal to or above IEF → BULLISH. This checks daily returns, not the raw price ratio against a moving average.

05

Copper

A cyclical industrial-metal proxy; weakening copper can signal softer growth expectations.

+1 max
BEARISH · +1

Daily copper price change is negative.

OTHER OUTCOMES · 0

Unchanged or rising copper → BULLISH.

06

VIX Curve

Compares short-dated VIX with three-month implied volatility (VIX3M). An inverted / backwardated curve can signal near-term stress.

+1 max
BEARISH · +1

VIX ÷ VIX3M is greater than 1.00.

OTHER OUTCOMES · 0

Below 0.90 → BULLISH (contango). 0.90 through 1.00 → CAUTION / shallow contango / flat; 1.00 is not bearish.

04 / REFERENCE ONLY

Data displayed but not scored

These add context without creating additional bearish points. In particular, a red informational label is not automatically an extra score point.

0 points

SPY / QQQ · 5, 20, 50, 100 and 200D SMAs

The simple moving average is the mean of the last N completed ETF closing prices. Each card displays its average and the percentage difference from the latest close.

BULLISH: latest close ≥ SMA (including equality). BEARISH: latest close < SMA. These individual comparisons add no points; the separate original Trend signal remains scored.

S&P Breadth · 5D

Five-session change in the percentage of S&P 500 constituents above their own 200D moving averages. ≤ −2 percentage points = CONTRACTING; ≥ +2 pp = EXPANDING; otherwise STABLE. Not in the score.

S&P Breadth · 200D level

Current percentage of S&P 500 constituents above their own 200D averages. Below 40% = BEARISH; 40% to less than 60% = CAUTION; ≥60% = BULLISH. Not in the score.

Nasdaq Breadth · 5D

Difference between the latest five-session average advancing share and the preceding five-session average, in percentage points. ≤ −5 pp = CONTRACTING; ≥ +5 pp = EXPANDING; otherwise STABLE. Not in the score.

RSI chart band vs. RSI score

RSI is scored once below 45, even though its chart also displays classic labels: ≤30 = OVERSOLD, ≥70 = OVERBOUGHT, and everything between = NEUTRAL. Those chart bands are not additional points.

SPY / QQQ charts & Fear & Greed

TradingView's hosted ETF price charts update independently of the EOD score engine. The separate FearGreedChart.com sentiment series is not the CNN index. Neither chart contributes bearish points. The website has no put/call ratio component.

05 / INTERPRETING THE SUMMARY

Beyond the 15-point total

The summary cards calculate trend, confirmations and swing environment separately from the 15 individual signal points.

1D / 5D score change

Today's bearish points minus the points one or five trading sessions earlier. Positive means risk signals increased; negative means they decreased.

For the 5D Score Trend: change ≥ +2 = DETERIORATING; change ≤ −2 = IMPROVING; otherwise STABLE. Missing feeds can cause UNCERTAIN rather than a misleading definite label.

Market Confirmation · 0/4 through 4/4

Four five-session recovery checks are counted: volatility declining (VIX for SPY, VXN for QQQ), HYG/IEF credit ratio rising, breadth improving (S&P 5D breadth ≥ +2 pp, or Nasdaq advancing-share five-session-window change ≥ +5 pp), and SPY / QQQ price rising. Unavailable categories count as unknown, not as failed.

Swing Environment

BULLISH: score ≤4, 5D change < +2, at least 3 confirmations, and trend not bearish. EARLY IMPROVEMENT: score ≥9, 5D change ≤−2 and at least 2 confirmations. BEARISH: score ≥9 without that early-improvement pattern. DETERIORATING: score 5–7 and 5D change ≥+2. Otherwise SELECTIVE.

Shows N/A if required information is missing or possible missing-input outcomes disagree; this is a separate decision aid, not an extra point.

Historical chart & data freshness

The historical line chart plots observed bearish points per available completed trading session (30 or 90 sessions). Weekends and holidays are compressed. An incomplete score has an uncertainty range even when only the observed count is plotted. Market inputs are end-of-day proxies and may differ from Thinkorswim data.

TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN

A score is a checklist, not a forecast.

This is an experimental model with heuristic thresholds. Signal overlap exists (for example, trend, RSI, breadth and sector leadership). Check the market data, timeframe, and indicator availability before using the score in a trading decision.
